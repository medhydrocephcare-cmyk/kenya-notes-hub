## 1. Nature and purpose of accounting

Accounting is the systematic process of **recording, classifying, summarising, and interpreting** financial transactions of a business so that its financial position and performance can be understood by those who need to make decisions about it.

### 1.1 Objectives of accounting

- To maintain a permanent, reliable record of every transaction.
- To determine profit or loss for a period.
- To ascertain the financial position (assets, liabilities, capital) at a point in time.
- To provide information for planning, control, and decision-making.
- To satisfy legal and tax reporting obligations.

### 1.2 Bookkeeping vs accounting

Bookkeeping is the mechanical, day-to-day recording of transactions. Accounting is broader — it includes bookkeeping, but also classification, summarising, analysis, and communication of financial information.

### 1.3 Users of accounting information

| User | Interest |
|---|---|
| Owner(s) | Profitability and return on their investment |
| Management | Day-to-day control and performance monitoring |
| Creditors/suppliers | Ability of the business to pay amounts owed |
| Government (KRA) | Correct assessment and payment of tax |
