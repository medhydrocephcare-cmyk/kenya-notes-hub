## 1. What is management?

**Management** is the process of working with and through people and other resources to accomplish an organisation's goals efficiently (minimum necessary resources) and effectively (actually achieving the intended goal).

---

## 2. The functions of management

The classical functions of management: **planning** (setting objectives and deciding how to achieve them), **organising** (arranging resources and tasks to implement the plan), **staffing** (recruiting and developing people), **directing/leading** (guiding and motivating staff toward objectives), and **controlling** (monitoring performance against plans and correcting deviations). These functions are continuous and overlapping in practice, not a strict one-time sequence.

---

## 3. Levels of management

- **Top management** — sets overall organisational direction and strategy (e.g. board, chief executive).
- **Middle management** — translates strategy into departmental plans, coordinating between top and lower management.
- **Lower (first-line) management** — directly supervises operational staff carrying out day-to-day tasks.

---

## 4. Organisational structures

- **Functional structure** — organised by specialised function (finance, marketing, operations); efficient for a single-focus organisation.
- **Divisional structure** — organised around products, services, or geographic markets; suits diversified organisations.
- **Matrix structure** — combines functional and divisional/project reporting lines; suits organisations needing flexibility across multiple projects, though it can create dual-reporting complexity.

---

## 5. Motivation theories

- **Maslow's hierarchy of needs** — people are motivated by satisfying needs in order: physiological, safety, social, esteem, and self-actualisation.
- **Herzberg's two-factor theory** — distinguishes **hygiene factors** (e.g. pay, working conditions — their absence causes dissatisfaction, but their presence doesn't necessarily motivate) from **motivators** (e.g. achievement, recognition, responsibility — genuinely drive motivation).
- **McGregor's Theory X and Theory Y** — Theory X assumes employees are inherently unmotivated and need close supervision; Theory Y assumes employees are self-motivated and seek responsibility — shaping very different management styles.

---

## 6. Leadership styles

- **Autocratic** — leader makes decisions unilaterally, with limited input from others.
- **Democratic (participative)** — leader involves team members in decision-making.
- **Laissez-faire** — leader gives significant autonomy, providing minimal direct oversight.

The most effective style generally depends on the situation — task urgency, team competence and experience, and the nature of the decision all influence which style works best in a given context.

---

## 7. Decision-making in management

A structured decision-making process: identify the problem → gather relevant information → identify possible alternatives → evaluate alternatives against relevant criteria → select and implement the best alternative → monitor and review the outcome. Good decision-making balances thorough analysis with the practical need to decide within a reasonable time frame — excessive analysis can itself become a costly delay.

---

## 8. Span of control and organisational design

**Span of control** is the number of subordinates a manager directly supervises. A **narrow span of control** allows closer supervision but requires more management layers (a "tall" structure); a **wide span of control** requires fewer management layers (a "flat" structure) but demands more delegation and subordinate capability, since a manager cannot supervise many people as closely.

---

## 9. Full worked question — span of control analysis

**Scenario.** A department has 60 staff members. Management is considering two structures: Structure A, with a span of control of 6 (each supervisor manages 6 staff), and Structure B, with a span of control of 12.

**Required:** (a) Compute the number of first-line supervisors required under each structure. (b) Explain one advantage and one disadvantage of Structure B compared to Structure A.

**Solution:**

(a) Structure A: 60 ÷ 6 = **10 supervisors**. Structure B: 60 ÷ 12 = **5 supervisors**.

(b) **Advantage of Structure B:** fewer supervisors are needed, reducing management overhead cost and creating a flatter, potentially faster-communicating structure. **Disadvantage of Structure B:** each supervisor has less time available per subordinate, which can reduce the quality of direct supervision and support unless subordinates are highly capable and require less close oversight.

## 12. Exam technique and revision checklist

- Always distinguish "efficiency" from "effectiveness" precisely with an example — a very commonly and specifically tested definition pair.
- Functions of management: know all five by name, and be ready to explain why they're continuous/overlapping, not sequential.
- Organisational structure "distinguish" questions: use consistent criteria (basis of organisation, suitability) on both sides.
- Motivation theory questions: know each theory's specific structure (Maslow's five levels; Herzberg's two factor types; McGregor's X/Y assumptions) precisely.
- Span of control questions: always show the division calculation explicitly, then explain the tall/flat structure trade-off in words.

**Quick revision — one line per topic:**
1. Management: working through people/resources to achieve goals efficiently and effectively.
2. Functions: planning, organising, staffing, directing, controlling — continuous and overlapping.
3. Levels: top (strategy), middle (departmental plans), lower (direct supervision).
4. Structures: functional (by function), divisional (by product/market), matrix (combined, more complex).
5. Maslow: physiological → safety → social → esteem → self-actualisation.
6. Herzberg: hygiene factors (prevent dissatisfaction) vs motivators (drive motivation).
7. Leadership: autocratic (unilateral), democratic (participative), laissez-faire (autonomous).
8. Span of control: narrow = tall structure/closer supervision; wide = flat structure/more delegation.
