## 1. The accounting equation and double entry

**Assets = Liabilities + Capital** must always balance. Every transaction is recorded via **double entry**: a debit and an equal, corresponding credit. Debits increase assets/expenses; credits increase liabilities/capital/income.

---

## 2. The trial balance and final accounts

A **trial balance** lists all ledger balances to check total debits equal total credits. **Final accounts** — the statement of profit or loss and the statement of financial position — summarise a company's performance and position for a period, typically presented to the board and shareholders as part of the company's annual reporting obligations.

---

## 3. Why a company secretary needs to understand accounting

A company secretary regularly handles board papers containing financial statements, coordinates the annual general meeting where accounts are presented to shareholders, and ensures statutory filing deadlines for financial statements are met. While not expected to prepare accounts personally, a company secretary who understands basic accounting can meaningfully support board discussion of financial matters and correctly identify statutory filing obligations tied to the company's financial position.

---

## 4. What is a tax, and why do governments levy them?

A **tax** is a compulsory financial charge imposed by government without a direct, specific benefit given in return, funding public services (infrastructure, security, healthcare, education) and serving as a policy tool (e.g. discouraging certain consumption, redistributing income).

---

## 5. Classification of taxes

- **Direct tax** — levied directly on income/profit (e.g. corporate income tax), borne by the person/entity taxed.
- **Indirect tax** — levied on goods/services (e.g. VAT), generally passed on to the final consumer through the price paid.
- **Progressive tax** — takes a larger percentage of income as income rises.
- **Regressive tax** — takes a larger percentage of income from lower earners than higher earners (common with flat-rate consumption taxes relative to income).

---

## 6. Kenya's tax administration — KRA

The **Kenya Revenue Authority (KRA)** is the government body responsible for assessing, collecting and accounting for tax revenue in Kenya, administering income tax, VAT, customs duties and other tax obligations, and enforcing compliance through audits, assessments and, where necessary, penalties for non-compliance.

---

## 7. Corporate tax obligations, basics

Companies in Kenya are generally subject to **corporate income tax** on taxable profits, along with obligations such as **Pay As You Earn (PAYE)** deductions from employee salaries, and **Value Added Tax (VAT)** registration/accounting where applicable to the company's turnover and activities. Meeting filing deadlines and maintaining proper supporting records are essential to compliance and avoiding penalties.

---

## 8. Tax compliance and the company secretary's role

While a company's finance function typically handles the technical tax computations, a company secretary often plays a supporting governance role: ensuring the board is properly informed of material tax obligations and risks, confirming statutory filing deadlines are tracked and met, and ensuring tax-related disclosures required in the company's statutory filings/annual reports are accurate and complete.

---

## 9. Full worked question — a simple final accounts adjustment

**Scenario.** A company's trial balance shows insurance expense of KSh 84,000, covering the 12 months from 1 April to 31 March. The company's financial year ends on 31 December.

**Required:** Compute the insurance expense for the year and the prepayment carried forward at year-end.

**Solution:**

Monthly insurance = 84,000 ÷ 12 = KSh 7,000 per month.

Expense relating to the year (1 April to 31 December) = 9 months × 7,000 = **KSh 63,000**.

Prepayment carried forward (1 January to 31 March, next year) = 3 months × 7,000 = **KSh 21,000**.

## 12. Exam technique and revision checklist

- Adjustment questions: carefully identify which months relate to the current period versus a prepayment/accrual before computing figures.
- Tax classification "distinguish" questions: use consistent criteria (who bears it, how it's levied) on both sides.
- KRA/tax administration questions: name specific functions (assess, collect, enforce) rather than a vague "manages tax."
- Company secretary's role questions (accounting/tax papers): always connect back to governance/compliance support, not technical tax computation, since that's the specific angle CS papers test.
- Always state the specific consequence (penalty, interest, reputational/regulatory risk) when asked about non-compliance, not just "it's bad for the company."
