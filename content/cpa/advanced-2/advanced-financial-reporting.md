## 1. Reconstruction and reorganisation of capital structure

**Purpose.** Reconstruction is a formal rearrangement of a company's capital, typically to eliminate accumulated losses, revalue assets to reflect current worth, and align the balance sheet with the future prospects of the business. It is not a rescue in the insolvency sense; it is a **going-concern restart** aimed at attracting new investment and clearing the balance sheet of overhang.

**Two broad routes:**
- **Internal reconstruction** — the same legal entity survives; share capital is reduced or reclassified, reserves reset, and liabilities compromised through a scheme of arrangement.
- **External reconstruction** — a new company acquires the assets and business of the old company, which is then wound up.

**Typical scheme steps (internal):**
1. Members and creditors approve the scheme (special resolution + court sanction if required).
2. Existing share capital is written down (e.g. KSh 100 shares reduced to KSh 20 fully paid).
3. Preference shareholders and debenture holders accept a haircut in exchange for new instruments.
4. Non-cash assets are revalued (upward where undervalued, downward where impaired).
5. Accumulated losses and any goodwill are written off against the capital reduction reserve.
6. New capital is issued to fund the restart.

**Capital Reduction Account** balances the scheme:
```
Cr with: reductions in share capital, cancellations, creditor sacrifices, upward revaluations
Dr with: accumulated losses, goodwill write-off, downward revaluations, expenses of scheme
```
Any surplus is transferred to a **Capital Reserve**; a deficit indicates the scheme is under-funded.

## 2. Accounting for income taxes (current and deferred)

**IAS 12** governs both current and deferred tax.

**Current tax** — the tax payable (or recoverable) on the taxable profit (loss) for the year, computed under prevailing tax law and rates enacted or substantively enacted by the reporting date. Charged to profit or loss unless it relates to items recognised in OCI or directly in equity.

**Deferred tax** arises from **temporary differences** between the carrying amount of an asset or liability in the balance sheet and its **tax base**.

**Two types of temporary difference:**
- **Taxable temporary differences** — will result in *taxable* amounts in future periods → **deferred tax liability**.
- **Deductible temporary differences** — will result in deductible amounts in future periods → **deferred tax asset** (subject to a recoverability test).

**Common sources:**
- Accelerated tax depreciation vs slower accounting depreciation → deferred tax liability.
- Provisions recognised for accounting but deductible only when paid (e.g. warranty, restructuring) → deferred tax asset.
- Revaluation of PPE in OCI → deferred tax on the revaluation surplus, recognised in OCI.
- Unused tax losses and unused tax credits → deferred tax asset only to the extent probable of recovery.

**Measurement.** At the tax rates expected to apply in the period the asset is realised or the liability is settled, based on rates enacted or substantively enacted by the reporting date. **Not discounted.**

**Presentation.** Deferred tax assets and liabilities are non-current. Offsetting is permitted only when strict conditions are met.

## 3. Impairment of assets (IAS 36)

**Principle.** An asset must not be carried at more than its **recoverable amount**. Recoverable amount is the **higher** of:

- **Fair value less costs of disposal (FVLCD)** — the price obtainable in an orderly transaction between market participants, less selling costs.
- **Value in use (VIU)** — the present value of the future cash flows expected from continuing use plus disposal at end of useful life.

**Indicators (external and internal).** External: market cap below net assets; decline in market value; adverse changes in technology, market, economy, or legal environment; interest rate rises. Internal: obsolescence or physical damage; asset held for disposal; internal reporting indicating economic performance worse than expected.

**Cash-generating units (CGUs).** Where an individual asset does not generate cash flows independently, impairment is tested at the CGU level. **Goodwill** is allocated to CGUs (or groups of CGUs) at acquisition and tested annually.

**Order of impairment write-down within a CGU:**
1. Goodwill first.
2. Then other assets pro-rata to their carrying amounts (subject to floor: no asset written below its individual recoverable amount).

**Reversal.** An impairment loss on an asset other than goodwill may be reversed when there is an indication the loss has decreased. **Goodwill impairment is never reversed.**

## 4. Earnings per share (IAS 33)

Two EPS figures are required for listed entities:

**Basic EPS**
```
Basic EPS = (Profit or loss attributable to ordinary equity holders of the parent)
            ÷ (Weighted average number of ordinary shares outstanding during the period)
```

Numerator adjustments: deduct preference dividends (cumulative — whether declared or not; non-cumulative — only if declared); deduct amounts relating to any equity instruments other than ordinary shares.

Denominator adjustments: time-weight new issues at fair value; retrospectively restate for bonus issues, share splits, and consolidations; for rights issues at less than fair value, apply the **theoretical ex-rights price (TERP)** adjustment factor.

**Diluted EPS** reflects the effect on EPS if all **dilutive potential ordinary shares** were converted. Adjust:
- Numerator — add back after-tax interest on convertible debt; add back preference dividends on convertible preferences.
- Denominator — add weighted number of shares that would result from conversion.

Only **dilutive** instruments are included; anti-dilutive ones are ignored. Test each instrument separately for dilution.

## 5. Share-based payment transactions (IFRS 2)

**Three categories:**
- **Equity-settled** — entity settles by issuing its own equity instruments. Recognise expense at **grant-date fair value** of the equity instruments; credit **equity**. No subsequent remeasurement of fair value; only revise estimates of the number of instruments that will vest.
- **Cash-settled** — entity settles in cash for an amount linked to the price of its equity (e.g. share appreciation rights). Recognise a **liability** measured at fair value at each reporting date until settlement; remeasurement changes go to profit or loss.
- **Choice of settlement** — treat as equity- or cash-settled depending on which party has the choice and past practice.

**Vesting conditions** split into:
- **Service conditions** and **non-market performance conditions** → affect the *number* of instruments expected to vest.
- **Market conditions** and **non-vesting conditions** → factored into the *grant-date fair value*; not re-estimated later.

**Illustration:**
```
Grant date: 1 Jan 2024. Options granted to 100 employees; 300 options each. Vesting: 3-year service.
Grant-date fair value per option: KSh 40. Expected forfeitures: 10% cumulative.

Year 1 cumulative expense = 100 × 300 × 40 × 90% × 1/3 = 360,000
```
Adjust each year for revised forfeiture estimates.

## 6. Group accounting overview

**IFRS 10 Consolidated Financial Statements** — a parent consolidates any entity it **controls**. Control has three elements: **power** over the investee, **exposure to variable returns**, and **ability to use power to affect returns**.

**IFRS 3 Business Combinations** — acquisition method: identify acquirer, determine acquisition date, recognise identifiable assets acquired and liabilities assumed at fair value, recognise goodwill (or bargain purchase gain).

**IAS 28 Investments in Associates and Joint Ventures** — significant influence (presumed at 20–50% voting rights) accounted for using the **equity method**.

**IFRS 11 Joint Arrangements** — split into **joint operations** (recognise share of assets, liabilities, revenues, and expenses) and **joint ventures** (equity method).

## 7. Consolidation of subsidiaries and sub-subsidiaries

**Direct subsidiary — mechanics:**
1. Determine acquirer, acquisition date, and consideration transferred.
2. Fair-value identifiable assets, liabilities, and contingent liabilities.
3. Compute goodwill (or bargain purchase):
   ```
   Consideration + NCI (at fair value OR proportionate share of net assets) − Fair value of net assets acquired
   ```
4. Consolidate line by line.
5. Eliminate intra-group balances, transactions, and unrealised profits.
6. Split post-acquisition profit between owners of parent and NCI.

**Sub-subsidiary (H → S → SS):**
- **Direct approach:** compute the parent's effective interest in SS (e.g. 80% × 60% = 48%), goodwill computed on that effective interest.
- **Indirect (step) approach:** consolidate SS into S first, then consolidate the combined entity into H. Both give the same net-asset figures but the NCI presentation differs — pick one and be consistent.

## 8. Associates and joint ventures

**Equity method:**
```
Opening investment
+ Share of post-acquisition profit (loss)
+ Share of OCI
− Dividends received from the associate
− Impairment of investment
= Closing carrying amount
```

**Key rules:**
- Adjust for fair-value differences at acquisition (e.g. additional depreciation on undervalued PPE).
- Eliminate the **investor's share** of unrealised profits on transactions with the associate (both upstream and downstream).
- Do not consolidate line by line — a single-line presentation.
- If losses reduce the carrying amount to nil, further losses are recognised only to the extent of any legal or constructive obligation to fund them.

## 9. Disposal of subsidiaries (full and partial)

**Full disposal.**
- Derecognise assets and liabilities of the subsidiary at their carrying amounts.
- Derecognise the NCI.
- Recognise proceeds and any retained investment at fair value.
- Recycle relevant amounts from OCI (e.g. FCTR, cash-flow hedge reserve) to profit or loss.
- Compute gain or loss on disposal in the consolidated P&L.

**Partial disposal — control retained.** Treated as an **equity transaction** (adjust NCI and equity; no gain or loss to P&L).

**Partial disposal — control lost.** Treated as a **derecognition** event: derecognise all assets/liabilities and NCI; recognise the retained interest at fair value; compute gain or loss.

**Disposal during the year.** Consolidate the subsidiary's results up to the disposal date, then remove it. The retained proportion of post-disposal results (if the retained interest is an associate) is equity-accounted for the rest of the year.

## 10. Piecemeal acquisitions

Stepwise increase in ownership:

- **Zero → significant influence (associate):** apply equity method from date significant influence obtained.
- **Associate → control (subsidiary):** deemed disposal of the associate at fair value plus acquisition of the subsidiary. Any gain/loss on the deemed disposal is recognised in P&L. Goodwill computed on the total (previously held stake at fair value + new consideration + NCI) less fair value of net assets.
- **Control → increased control:** equity transaction — no P&L impact; adjust NCI and parent equity.

## 11. Consolidation of foreign subsidiaries (IAS 21)

**Functional currency** — the currency of the primary economic environment in which the entity operates. **Presentation currency** — the currency of the group's consolidated financial statements.

**Translation rules for a foreign subsidiary whose functional currency differs from the group's presentation currency:**
- **Assets and liabilities** — at **closing rate** at the reporting date.
- **Income and expenses** — at rates prevailing at the transaction dates (average rate is acceptable if reasonable).
- **Equity items** — at historical rates.
- **Exchange differences** — recognised in **OCI** and accumulated in a **Foreign Currency Translation Reserve (FCTR)**.

On disposal of the foreign subsidiary the cumulative FCTR is **recycled to profit or loss** as part of the gain/loss on disposal.

**Goodwill** on a foreign subsidiary is treated as an asset of the foreign operation and translated at the closing rate.

## 12. Cash flow statements (IAS 7)

**Three activities:**
- **Operating** — principal revenue-producing activities; primary indicator of cash-generating ability.
- **Investing** — acquisition/disposal of long-term assets and other investments.
- **Financing** — changes in the size and composition of contributed equity and borrowings.

**Two operating methods:**
- **Direct method** — presents major classes of gross cash receipts and payments.
- **Indirect method** — reconciles profit before tax to operating cash flow through non-cash adjustments and working-capital movements.

**Group cash flow specifics:**
- Eliminate dividends received *from* subsidiaries within the group; dividends from associates appear in *investing*.
- Cash paid to acquire subsidiaries, net of cash acquired, appears in *investing*.
- Interest and dividends paid may be classified as either operating or financing (be consistent).
- For **banks**, most interest and trading cash flows are operating.
- For **insurers**, premium receipts and claim payments are operating.

**Note disclosures**: non-cash investing and financing transactions (e.g. asset acquired under finance lease, conversion of debt to equity).

## 13. Retirement benefits (IAS 19)

Two plan types:

**Defined contribution (DC).** Employer's obligation limited to agreed contribution. Recognise contribution as expense when service is rendered. Simple.

**Defined benefit (DB).** Employer commits to a specified pension outcome; carries actuarial and investment risk.

Recognise a **net defined benefit liability (asset)**:
```
Present value of DB obligation (using projected unit credit method)
Less: Fair value of plan assets
= Net liability (asset)
```

**Components of DB cost:**
- **Service cost** (current + past service + gains/losses on settlements) → **P&L**.
- **Net interest** on the net liability/asset (discount rate × opening net position) → **P&L**.
- **Remeasurements** (actuarial gains/losses on the obligation; return on plan assets excluding amounts in net interest; changes in asset ceiling effects) → **OCI**, never recycled.

**Past service cost** from plan amendments is recognised in full at the earlier of the amendment date and the date any related restructuring cost is recognised.

## 14. Segmental reporting (IFRS 8)

**Management approach.** Report segments as they are viewed **internally** by the **chief operating decision-maker (CODM)**.

**Quantitative thresholds** — an operating segment is reportable if it meets any of:
- Reported revenue (external + intersegment) ≥ 10% of total revenue.
- Absolute profit or loss ≥ 10% of the greater of combined profit of profitable segments and combined loss of loss-making segments.
- Assets ≥ 10% of total assets.

Combined reportable segments must represent ≥ **75%** of external revenue; add more segments until threshold met.

**Required disclosures per segment:** revenue (external and intersegment), profit or loss, assets and liabilities (if regularly reported to CODM), plus reconciliations to consolidated totals. Also required: entity-wide disclosures on products/services, geography, and major customers.

## 15. Banking and insurance reporting essentials

**Banks (IFRS 9, IAS 32, IFRS 7):**
- Financial assets classified based on business model + SPPI test → amortised cost, FVOCI, or FVTPL.
- Impairment on financial assets: **expected credit loss (ECL)** model — 12-month ECL vs lifetime ECL (three-stage model).
- Extensive disclosures on credit risk, liquidity risk, market risk, and capital adequacy.

**Insurance (IFRS 17):**
- Groups insurance contracts into portfolios and measures liabilities using the **general model** (building block approach), the **premium allocation approach** (short-duration, PAA), or the **variable fee approach** (direct participating contracts).
- Recognise **contractual service margin (CSM)** — unearned profit released over coverage period.
- Discount cash flows and adjust for risk.

## 16. Self-assessment question bank (original)

**Q1.** Distinguish internal from external reconstruction. *(3 marks)*

*Model answer:* Internal — same legal entity survives; capital reduced/reclassified and losses written off against a Capital Reduction Account, backed by court sanction. External — new company acquires assets and business of the old, which is wound up.

**Q2.** Explain the difference between a taxable temporary difference and a deductible temporary difference. *(3 marks)*

*Model answer:* Taxable temporary difference will produce taxable amounts in future periods → deferred tax liability. Deductible temporary difference will produce deductible amounts in future periods → deferred tax asset, recognised only to the extent probable of recovery.

**Q3.** Define recoverable amount for the purposes of IAS 36. *(2 marks)*

*Model answer:* The higher of fair value less costs of disposal and value in use of the asset (or CGU).

**Q4.** State three numerator or denominator adjustments required in computing diluted EPS from basic EPS. *(3 marks)*

*Model answer:* Add back after-tax interest on convertible debt; add back preference dividends on convertible preference shares (numerator). Add weighted shares issuable on assumed conversion; adjust for options using the treasury-stock-style method (denominator). Exclude anti-dilutive instruments.

**Q5.** For an equity-settled share-based payment, explain how market vs non-market performance conditions are treated. *(4 marks)*

*Model answer:* Non-market performance conditions and service conditions affect the *number* of instruments expected to vest — re-estimated at each reporting date. Market conditions are built into the *grant-date fair value* and are not re-estimated for changes in outcome.

**Q6.** State the three elements of control under IFRS 10. *(3 marks)*

*Model answer:* Power over the investee; exposure to variable returns; ability to use power to affect returns.

**Q7.** Explain the accounting for a partial disposal of a subsidiary where control is retained. *(3 marks)*

*Model answer:* Treated as an equity transaction: no gain or loss to P&L; NCI is adjusted upward and any difference between consideration received and the change in NCI is taken directly to parent equity.

**Q8.** Outline the translation approach for a foreign subsidiary under IAS 21. *(4 marks)*

*Model answer:* Assets and liabilities at closing rate; income and expenses at transaction-date (or average) rates; equity at historical rates; exchange differences to OCI accumulating in the FCTR; recycled to P&L on disposal. Goodwill treated as an asset of the foreign operation and translated at closing rate.

**Q9.** State the three components of defined benefit cost and where each is presented. *(3 marks)*

*Model answer:* Service cost — P&L. Net interest — P&L. Remeasurements — OCI (not recycled).

**Q10.** State the quantitative thresholds used to identify a reportable operating segment under IFRS 8. *(3 marks)*

*Model answer:* Revenue ≥ 10% of total; absolute profit or loss ≥ 10% of the greater of combined profits or combined losses; assets ≥ 10% of total. Add reportable segments until they cover at least 75% of external revenue.

## 17. Exam technique and revision checklist

- **Cite the IFRS** by number in every technical answer (IAS 12, IAS 21, IAS 33, IAS 36, IFRS 2, IFRS 8, IFRS 10, IFRS 3, IAS 19). One correct citation is often the difference between B and A.
- On group accounts, always identify **who is the acquirer**, the **acquisition date**, and how NCI is measured (fair value vs proportionate share) — these three choices drive the rest.
- On EPS, do bonus issues, splits and consolidations **retrospectively**; time-weight new issues at fair value.
- On deferred tax, list the temporary differences **before** doing the calculation — an unlabelled figure loses marks.
- On IAS 36, remember **goodwill first, then pro-rata**, and never reverse goodwill impairment.
- On IFRS 2, treat market vs non-market conditions **explicitly** — most script errors happen at this junction.
- On foreign subsidiaries, do not forget to **recycle the FCTR** to P&L on disposal.
- On cash flows, present the **three sections in order** and disclose non-cash transactions in the notes.
- On segments, present the **quantitative thresholds** and note the **75% rule**.

**One-line revision recap:**
1. Reconstruction rebalances the balance sheet; scheme flows through a Capital Reduction Account.
2. Deferred tax = tax base vs carrying amount, at future rates, not discounted.
3. Impairment: recoverable = higher of FVLCD and VIU; goodwill first, no reversal.
4. Basic EPS uses weighted shares; diluted adds dilutive potentials only.
5. IFRS 2: equity-settled fair value at grant; cash-settled remeasured to fair value each year.
6. IFRS 10 control = power + exposure to variable returns + ability to affect returns.
7. Sub-subsidiary: effective interest for goodwill.
8. Associate = equity method; unrealised profits share eliminated.
9. Partial disposal: control kept = equity transaction; control lost = derecognise + gain/loss.
10. IAS 21: closing rate for B/S; average for P&L; differences in OCI (FCTR) recycled on disposal.
11. IAS 19: split DB cost into service, net interest (P&L) and remeasurements (OCI).
12. IFRS 8: management approach + 10% and 75% thresholds.
13. IFRS 9: business model + SPPI drives classification; ECL drives impairment.
14. IFRS 17: general model + CSM releases profit over coverage.
