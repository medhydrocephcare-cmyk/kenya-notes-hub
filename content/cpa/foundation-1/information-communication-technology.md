## 1. Computer hardware and software

**Hardware** is the physical, tangible components of a computer (input devices like a keyboard/mouse, output devices like a monitor/printer, the CPU, and storage devices). **Software** is the intangible instructions directing hardware — **system software** (e.g. the operating system, managing hardware and providing the platform for other software) and **application software** (programs for specific tasks, e.g. spreadsheets or accounting packages).

---

## 2. Data versus information

**Data** is raw, unprocessed facts with no inherent meaning until organised (e.g. a list of individual transactions). **Information** is data that has been processed, organised and structured to become meaningful for decision-making (e.g. a monthly management report). Good information should be accurate, timely, relevant, complete, and appropriately presented for its intended user.

---

## 3. Application software for accounting and business

- **Spreadsheets** — organising data into rows/columns, with built-in formulas, widely used for budgets and calculations.
- **Accounting/ERP packages** — recording transactions and generating financial statements, often integrating multiple business functions (finance, inventory, payroll) into one system.
- **Word processors and presentation software** — documents and reports; slide-based presentations for meetings.
- **Database software** — storing and retrieving structured data efficiently, useful for larger volumes of customer or transaction records than a spreadsheet can conveniently manage.

---

## 4. Spreadsheets — formulas and functions

A spreadsheet organises data into a grid of rows and columns, each intersection called a **cell**. Common functions: `=SUM(range)` totals a range of cells; `=AVERAGE(range)` computes the mean; `=IF(condition, value_if_true, value_if_false)` applies conditional logic (e.g. flagging overdue accounts). Spreadsheets are widely used in accounting-support work for budgets, simple ledger summaries, and "what-if" analysis (changing one input to see the effect on a result, e.g. testing how a change in sales volume affects profit).

---

## 5. Databases, basics

A **database** is an organised collection of structured data, typically arranged into **tables** (rows and columns), allowing efficient storage, retrieval and querying of large volumes of information — for example, a customer database storing each customer's details and transaction history, queryable to answer specific questions (e.g. "which customers have overdue balances above KSh 50,000?") far more efficiently than searching a large spreadsheet manually.

---

## 6. Computer networks and the internet

A **network** connects computers to share data, software and hardware resources. A **LAN** covers a small area (e.g. one office); a **WAN** covers a large area (the internet being the largest WAN). The internet enables business communication, research, online banking and e-commerce; **email** remains one of the most widely used business communication tools, valued for speed and a written record, but requiring care around tone and security (phishing).

---

## 7. Information and data security

Key measures: **passwords/access controls** (restricting system access to authorised users), **antivirus/anti-malware software**, **regular backups** (enabling recovery if data is lost or corrupted, including from ransomware), **firewalls** (controlling network traffic), and **user awareness training** (since human error, e.g. falling for phishing or sharing passwords, is a common cause of security breaches alongside technical weaknesses).

---

## 8. Accounting information systems, introduction

An **accounting information system (AIS)** is the combination of people, processes, and technology an organisation uses to collect, record, process and report financial data. A well-designed AIS incorporates internal controls (e.g. restricting who can post journal entries, requiring approval for payments above a threshold) directly into the system's design, reducing reliance on manual checking alone and helping ensure the accuracy and integrity of the financial data it produces.

## 9. Full worked question — spreadsheet formulas for a simple ledger summary

**Scenario.** A spreadsheet lists four months of expenses in cells D2 to D5: 18,000; 21,500; 16,200; 19,800 (KSh).

**Required:** (a) Write the formula for cell D6 to compute total expenses. (b) Write the formula for cell D7 to compute average monthly expenses. (c) Compute the resulting values.

**Solution:**

(a) `=SUM(D2:D5)`

(b) `=AVERAGE(D2:D5)`

(c) Total = 18,000+21,500+16,200+19,800 = **KSh 75,500**. Average = 75,500 ÷ 4 = **KSh 18,875**.

## 11. Marking guide — model answers to the practice examination

**QUESTION ONE**

(a) *(12 marks)* — Hardware: physical components (e.g. keyboard, monitor). Software: instructions/programs directing hardware (e.g. operating system, spreadsheet program) — 6 marks each for definition plus two examples.

(b) *(8 marks)* — Data: raw unprocessed facts (e.g. a list of transactions). Information: processed, organised data that is meaningful for decision-making (e.g. a monthly report).

**QUESTION TWO**

(a) *(3 marks)* — `=SUM(E2:E6)`

(b) *(3 marks)* — `=AVERAGE(E2:E6)`

(c) *(6 marks)* — Total = 30,000+45,000+28,000+50,000+32,000 = **KSh 185,000**. Average = 185,000 ÷ 5 = **KSh 37,000**.

(d) *(8 marks — 2 marks each)* — Spreadsheet (budgets/calculations); accounting/ERP package (recording transactions); word processor (documents); presentation software (slides for meetings) — database software also acceptable.

**QUESTION THREE**

(a) *(10 marks)* — A database is an organised collection of structured data, typically in tables, allowing efficient storage, retrieval and querying; advantages over a spreadsheet: more efficient querying/filtering of large data volumes, and better handling of relationships between different data sets (e.g. customers and their transactions) — any two, 3 marks each, plus 4 for the definition.

(b) *(6 marks)* — A LAN covers a small geographic area (e.g. one office); a WAN covers a large geographic area (the internet being the largest WAN).

(c) *(4 marks — 2 marks each)* — Risk of phishing/malicious attachments; risk of miscommunication due to absent tone/non-verbal cues.

**QUESTION FOUR**

(a) *(16 marks — 4 marks each)* — Passwords/access controls, antivirus/anti-malware, regular backups, firewalls.

(b) *(4 marks)* — Because human error (e.g. falling for phishing, sharing passwords) is a common cause of security breaches, so trained, alert users are an important defence alongside technical controls.

**QUESTION FIVE**

(a) *(6 marks)* — An AIS is the combination of people, processes and technology an organisation uses to collect, record, process and report financial data.

(b) *(8 marks)* — Internal controls can be built directly into an AIS's design, e.g. requiring a second-level approval within the system before a payment above a set threshold can be processed, reducing reliance on manual checking alone.

(c) *(6 marks — 3 marks each)* — Accurate, timely (relevant or complete also acceptable).

## 12. Exam technique and revision checklist

- Hardware vs software: always pair the definition with a concrete example — a bare definition alone often earns partial marks only.
- Spreadsheet formula questions: write the exact formula syntax, not a description of the calculation in words.
- Data vs information: remember information is data that has been processed/organised to become meaningful — a very commonly tested distinction.
- Security questions: name specific measures (passwords, antivirus, backups, firewalls) rather than a vague "keep it secure."
- AIS questions: always give a concrete example when asked to explain how a control is built into a system, not just an abstract description.

**Quick revision — one line per topic:**
1. Hardware = physical components; software = instructions/programs (system vs application software).
2. Data = raw unprocessed facts; information = processed, meaningful data.
3. Spreadsheet functions: `=SUM(range)`, `=AVERAGE(range)`, `=IF(condition, true, false)`.
4. Database: organised, structured data in tables, efficient for querying large volumes.
5. LAN = small area network; WAN = large area network (internet is the largest WAN).
6. Security measures: passwords/access control, antivirus, backups, firewalls, user awareness training.
7. AIS: people + processes + technology collecting/recording/processing/reporting financial data.
8. Good information: accurate, timely, relevant, complete, appropriately presented.
