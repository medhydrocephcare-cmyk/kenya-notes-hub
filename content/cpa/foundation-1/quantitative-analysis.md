## 1. Basic algebra and linear equations

Many quantitative business problems reduce to a linear equation that can be solved by isolating the unknown. For example, given Total cost = Fixed cost + (Variable cost per unit × Units), if total cost, fixed cost and variable cost per unit are known, the number of units can be found by:

Units = (Total cost − Fixed cost) ÷ Variable cost per unit

---

## 2. Simple and compound interest

**Simple interest:** I = P × R × T (Principal × Rate × Time), calculated only on the original principal each period.

**Compound interest:** A = P × (1 + r)ⁿ, where interest is calculated on the principal plus previously added interest — "interest on interest" — so compound amounts grow faster than simple interest over time.

---

## 3. Annuities, basics

An **annuity** is a series of equal periodic payments. The present value of an ordinary annuity (payments at the end of each period) can be computed as:

PV = A × [1 − (1 + r)⁻ⁿ] ÷ r

where **A** is the periodic payment, **r** is the interest rate per period, and **n** is the number of periods. Annuities are commonly used to model loan repayments, regular savings plans, and lease payments.

---

## 4. Measures of central tendency

- **Mean** — sum of values ÷ number of values; sensitive to outliers.
- **Median** — the middle value of ordered data (or average of the two middle values for an even count); unaffected by outliers.
- **Mode** — the most frequently occurring value.

---

## 5. Measures of dispersion

- **Range** — highest value minus lowest value; simple but uses only two data points.
- **Variance** — the average of the squared deviations of each value from the mean.
- **Standard deviation** — the square root of the variance; measures how spread out values are around the mean, in the same units as the original data.

---

## 6. Index numbers

A **price index** measures the relative change in price over time compared to a fixed base period (set to 100):

Price index = (Price in current period ÷ Price in base period) × 100

An index of 112 means the value has risen 12% relative to the base period.

---

## 7. Basic probability

Probability of an event = Number of favourable outcomes ÷ Total number of possible outcomes, ranging from 0 (impossible) to 1 (certain). For mutually exclusive events, the probability that either occurs is the sum of their individual probabilities. For independent events, the probability that both occur is the product of their individual probabilities.

---

## 8. Simple linear regression and correlation, introduction

**Correlation** measures the strength and direction of a linear relationship between two variables, typically expressed as a **correlation coefficient (r)** ranging from −1 (perfect negative relationship) through 0 (no linear relationship) to +1 (perfect positive relationship). **Simple linear regression** fits a straight line (y = a + bx) to describe how a dependent variable (y) tends to change as an independent variable (x) changes, used for description and, cautiously, for forecasting within the range of the observed data.

## 9. Full worked question — annuity and standard deviation

**Scenario.** (a) Compute the present value of an ordinary annuity paying KSh 50,000 per year for 3 years, at a discount rate of 10% per year. (b) A data set of five values has a mean of 20. The squared deviations from the mean are: 4, 9, 1, 16, 0. Compute the variance and standard deviation.

**Solution:**

(a) PV = 50,000 × [1 − (1.10)⁻³] ÷ 0.10 = 50,000 × [1 − 0.751315] ÷ 0.10 = 50,000 × 2.48685 = **KSh 124,343** (approximately).

(b) Variance = Sum of squared deviations ÷ Number of values = (4+9+1+16+0) ÷ 5 = 30 ÷ 5 = **6**.

Standard deviation = √6 = **≈ 2.449**.

(a) *(12 marks)* — PV = 40,000 × [1 − (1.12)⁻⁴] ÷ 0.12 = 40,000 × [1 − 0.635518] ÷ 0.12 = 40,000 × 3.03735 = **KSh 121,494** (approximately).

**QUESTION THREE**

(a) *(5 marks)* — Mean = (40+35+40+50+45+40) ÷ 6 = 250 ÷ 6 = **KSh 41,667** (approximately).

(b) *(5 marks)* — Ordered: 35,40,40,40,45,50 → Median = (40+40) ÷ 2 = **KSh 40,000**.

(d) *(6 marks)* — Range = 50 − 35 = **KSh 15,000**.

**QUESTION FOUR**

(a) *(8 marks)* — Price index = (75 ÷ 60) × 100 = **125**; the price has risen by 25% relative to the base year.

**QUESTION FIVE**

(a) *(10 marks)* — A correlation coefficient measures the strength and direction of a linear relationship between two variables, ranging from −1 to +1; a value of −0.9 indicates a strong negative (inverse) linear relationship — as one variable increases, the other tends to decrease strongly.

(b) *(10 marks)* — Simple linear regression fits a straight line describing how a dependent variable changes as an independent variable changes, useful for description and cautious forecasting; a key caution is that forecasting outside the range of the original observed data (extrapolation) can be unreliable, since the relationship may not hold beyond the data actually observed.

## 12. Exam technique and revision checklist

- Word-problem/equation questions: always write the equation explicitly before solving.
- Interest questions: state clearly whether simple or compound, and use the matching formula.
- Annuity questions: state the formula explicitly and be careful with the negative exponent in (1+r)⁻ⁿ.
- Central tendency/dispersion questions: order the data first, and double check for ties in the mode.
- Correlation/regression questions: always interpret the number in plain words (strength AND direction) when asked to interpret, not just state the formula.

**Quick revision — one line per topic:**
1. Simple interest: I = PRT. Compound interest: A = P(1+r)ⁿ.
2. Annuity PV = A × [1 − (1+r)⁻ⁿ] ÷ r.
3. Mean = sum ÷ count; median = middle value (ordered); mode = most frequent value.
4. Range = highest − lowest; variance = average squared deviation from mean; standard deviation = √variance.
5. Price index = (Current price ÷ Base price) × 100.
6. Probability = favourable outcomes ÷ total outcomes; independent events multiply, mutually exclusive events add.
