## 1. Why tax disputes arise

Kenya operates a **self-assessment** tax system: the taxpayer computes tax, files the return, and pays. The Commissioner is *not* bound by that self-assessment and may re-open matters within the statutory window. Disagreement between taxpayer and Commissioner produces a **tax dispute**.

Common triggers:
- Disputed *taxable income* (what should be included in gross income).
- Disputed *allowable expenses* (whether a deduction is legitimate).
- Imposition of *penalties and interest* — or refusal by the Commissioner / Cabinet Secretary to waive them.
- *Overpayment* claims that the Commissioner rejects.
- Divergent *interpretations of tax law*.
- General *dissatisfaction with administrative action* (e.g. delayed refund, wrongful demand).

A dispute is formally deemed to have commenced when the taxpayer lodges a **notice of objection** against a "tax decision" of the Commissioner under the TPA.

## 3. Stage 1 — internal dispute resolution (objection to the Commissioner)

The taxpayer must lodge a **notice of objection** in writing with the Commissioner **within 30 days** of being notified of the tax decision.

**Requirements of a valid objection:**
- The taxpayer must have **paid the tax not in dispute**, or applied for an extension to do so.
- All relevant supporting *documents* must be attached.
- The notice must state **precisely**:
  - the grounds of objection,
  - the amendments the taxpayer wants made, and
  - the reasons for those amendments.

**Commissioner's response — within 60 days of receipt** of the objection, one of three outcomes:
1. **Amend** the assessment in line with the objection;
2. **Amend** the assessment to the extent the Commissioner considers appropriate (partial); or
3. **Decline** to amend the assessment (objection decision confirmed).

If the Commissioner does not respond within the 60-day window, the objection is generally treated as *allowed* — but always confirm the current statutory position, since this rule has been amended over time.

---

## 4. Stage 2 — appeal to the Tax Appeals Tribunal (TAT)

Governed by the **Tax Appeals Tribunal Act, 2013 (TATA)**. A person **dissatisfied with an appealable decision** may appeal to the TAT.

**Precondition:** the appellant must have **paid the tax not in dispute** (or agreed a payment arrangement with the Commissioner). Failing this, the appeal is invalid.

**Filing procedure:**
1. File a **notice of appeal** in writing with the Tribunal **within 30 days** of receiving the Commissioner's objection decision.
2. Pay the prescribed non-refundable filing fee (illustrative: KSh 20,000 — verify current fee).
3. Within **14 days** of filing the notice, lodge with the Clerk sufficient copies of:
   - the **memorandum of appeal**,
   - the **statement of facts**, and
   - the **tax decision** being appealed.
4. **Serve** a copy of the appeal on the Commissioner within **2 days** of filing.
5. The appellant is generally **restricted to the grounds** raised in the appeal (the Tribunal may allow new grounds only in defined circumstances).
6. The Tribunal must **hear and determine** the appeal **within 90 days** of filing.

---

## 5. Stage 3 — appeal to the High Court

Either party may appeal a TAT decision to the **High Court** within **30 days** (extendable at the Court's discretion). Appeals are typically limited to **points of law**, not re-litigation of facts.

**Filing checklist:**
- Notice of appeal served on the respondent within the statutory window.
- Memorandum of appeal presented to the Registrar within 30 days of service of that notice.
- A copy of the memorandum served on the respondent.
- Documents to file with the memorandum:
  - a copy of the decision (or the notice of decision) being appealed;
  - a copy of the notice of appeal already served; and
  - a statement (signed by the appellant or their advocate) setting out the facts on which the appeal is based.

---

## 6. Stage 4 — appeal to the Court of Appeal

Section 54 of the TPA permits a party dissatisfied with the High Court's decision to appeal to the **Court of Appeal** within **30 days** (extendable). Typical grounds:

- The decision is **contrary to law** or to some usage having the force of law.
- The decision failed to determine a **material issue of law or usage**.
- The decision was based on a **substantial error or defect** in procedure that may have produced a material error or miscarriage of justice.

The Court of Appeal's decision on a tax appeal is generally final for KRA-tax purposes (constitutional questions may still travel to the Supreme Court).

## 7. Alternative Dispute Resolution (ADR) — legal basis and objectives

Prior to 2015, most unresolved tax disputes ended in prolonged litigation. On **17 June 2015** KRA launched an **ADR Framework** to allow faster, cheaper, and confidential settlement outside the Tribunal and Courts.

**Legal footing:** Section 55 of the TPA anchors ADR by giving it a **90-day window** to conclude matters pending before the Tribunal or Court; where the Court itself refers a matter to ADR, timelines follow the Court's direction.

**Definition:** ADR is a **voluntary, facilitated mediation** between the taxpayer and the Commissioner, conducted in the presence of a **facilitator** (mediator), and — where the matter is already before TAT or a court — with the leave of that forum.

**Objectives:**
- Cut the time and cost of resolving disputes.
- Preserve the commercial relationship between KRA and the taxpayer.
- Reduce backlog at the Tribunal and courts.
- Produce a **win–win** settlement negotiated by both parties.
- Provide a **confidential** environment for candid discussion.

---

## 8. Types of disputes eligible for ADR

All applications go through a **suitability test** before admission. Eligible categories include:

- Disputes where the tax assessment has **not yet been confirmed** (still at objection stage).
- Disputes where the assessment **has been confirmed** but both parties agree to a **self-review**.
- Disputes already before the **Tax Appeals Tribunal or the Courts** where both parties want an **out-of-court settlement**.

ADR can cover disputes arising under any of the tax laws — Income Tax, VAT, Excise, and Customs duty.

---

## 9. Benefits of ADR

- **Speed** — 90-day statutory ceiling for pending court/tribunal matters.
- **Lower cost** — no lengthy litigation fees.
- **Confidentiality** — proceedings and outcomes are not public record.
- **Preserves relationships** — commercial ties between the taxpayer and KRA are maintained.
- **Win–win outcomes** — the final position is *negotiated*, not imposed.
- **Reduces backlog** at TAT and the courts.
- **Flexibility** — parties can craft creative settlement terms (payment plans, phased releases, etc.) that a court cannot easily order.
- **Certainty** — signed ADR agreement is binding and represents full and final settlement of the dispute.

---

## 10. Parties to ADR and their roles

There are **three** parties to a typical ADR:

**(1) The taxpayer** — natural person, company, association, or body of persons with a live tax dispute.

**(2) The Commissioner**, represented by the officer(s) involved in the underlying audit or investigation. This can be officers from:
- Customs & Border Control Department (C&BC),
- Domestic Taxes Department (DTD),
- Intelligence & Strategic Operations Department (I&SO), or
- Investigations & Enforcement Department (I&E).

**(3) The Facilitator** — the mediator who chairs the ADR sittings. Need not be a tax or legal expert.

**Role of the Facilitator:**
- Convene ADR meetings.
- Chair sittings and provide administrative guidance.
- Attest to the signing of the ADR Agreement.
- Manage timelines and ensure ground rules are observed.

**Obligations of the parties:**
- Attend all scheduled meetings and remain committed to the process.
- Give sufficient notice with reasons if unable to attend, and send a suitable proxy.
- Avoid disruptive conduct during meetings (e.g. telephone calls).
- Strictly observe agreed timelines, subject to reasonable exceptions.

---

## 11. Issues **excluded** from ADR

ADR is *not* suitable where:

- The settlement would be **contrary to the Constitution**, the revenue laws, or any other enabling law.
- The dispute turns on **technical interpretation of law** (needs a judicial ruling).
- **Public interest** requires judicial clarification of the issue.
- There are **existing undisputed judgments or rulings** on point.
- One party is **unwilling** to engage in ADR discussions.

---

## 12. ADR agreement — terms, timelines, and binding effect

The ADR settlement agreement typically records:

1. Background of the dispute.
2. **Agreed and non-agreed issues.**
3. The processes and specific exercises undertaken during ADR.
4. Any working meetings held (including any without the ADR team, e.g. reconciliations).
5. Taxes payable and the justification for the amount.
6. Terms of settlement.
7. Undertakings by each party.
8. **Payment plans** where applicable.
9. Signatures of both parties (or their representatives).
10. Witnessed by the Facilitator.
11. Each party retains a copy.
12. The signed agreement is **binding** and constitutes **full and final settlement** of the dispute.

**Statutory timeline (Section 55 TPA):** for disputes already before TAT or a court, ADR must conclude within **90 days**; where the court initiated the referral, timelines follow the court's directions.

---

## 13. Termination of ADR

ADR may be terminated where:

- The parties **fail to reach agreement** within the 90-day window (or extended window granted by the court).
- One party **withdraws** from the process.
- The dispute is found on further examination to be **unsuitable** for ADR (e.g. it turns out to raise a technical interpretation of law).
- The facilitator determines that the process cannot progress in good faith.

On termination, the matter reverts to the original forum (objection stage, TAT, or court) and proceeds as if ADR had not been invoked.

## 14. Full worked scenario — walking through the stages

**Scenario (original).** *Kilimo Fresh Ltd*, a fruit exporter, is issued an additional corporation-tax assessment on **1 March 2026** for KSh 18 million relating to disallowed export-marketing expenses for the year of income 2024. The company accepts KSh 4 million of the assessment but disputes KSh 14 million.

**Required:** Outline the steps and key deadlines Kilimo Fresh Ltd should follow through the tax dispute resolution mechanism.

**Model answer**

*Step 1 — pay the undisputed tax*
- By 1 March 2026 (or shortly thereafter), pay the KSh 4 million not in dispute, or apply for an extension to pay it.

*Step 2 — lodge notice of objection (by 30 March 2026)*
- Within 30 days of the assessment, file a written notice of objection stating the precise grounds (e.g. the disallowed expenses were incurred wholly and exclusively for producing taxable income), the amendment sought (allow the KSh 14 million deduction), and the reasons. Attach supporting invoices, contracts, and evidence of payment.

*Step 3 — await Commissioner's response (within 60 days)*
- The Commissioner may allow, partially allow, or decline the objection. Say the Commissioner declines on 15 May 2026.

*Step 4 — consider ADR*
- Kilimo Fresh Ltd may, in parallel or before escalating, apply for ADR (subject to the suitability test). If admitted, mediation must conclude within 90 days.

*Step 5 — appeal to TAT (by 14 June 2026)*
- If ADR is unsuccessful or not pursued, file a notice of appeal to the Tribunal within 30 days of the objection decision, pay the filing fee, and file the memorandum of appeal, statement of facts, and copy of the tax decision within 14 days. Serve on the Commissioner within 2 days. The Tribunal hears and determines within 90 days.

*Step 6 — High Court (within 30 days of TAT decision)*
- If dissatisfied with the TAT decision on a point of law, appeal to the High Court.

*Step 7 — Court of Appeal (within 30 days of High Court decision)*
- If still dissatisfied, appeal on the statutory grounds (contrary to law; failure to determine a material issue; substantial procedural defect).

At each stage, the taxpayer must continue to pay any *undisputed* portion of the tax to keep the appeal valid.

## 15. Self-assessment question bank (original)

**Q1.** List any four common causes of tax disputes between a taxpayer and the Commissioner. *(4 marks)*

*Model answer:* Disputed taxable income; disputed allowable expenses; imposition of penalties and interest or refusal to waive them; overpayment claims; interpretation of tax laws; dissatisfaction with administrative action. *(Any 4.)*

**Q2.** State the requirements of a valid notice of objection. *(3 marks)*

*Model answer:* Lodged in writing within 30 days of the tax decision; taxpayer has paid (or arranged to pay) the tax not in dispute; supporting documents attached; grounds, amendments sought, and reasons precisely stated.

**Q3.** State the three possible outcomes of the Commissioner's response to an objection. *(3 marks)*

*Model answer:* (i) Amend the assessment in line with the objection; (ii) amend to the extent the Commissioner considers appropriate; (iii) decline to amend.

**Q4.** Outline the procedural steps for filing an appeal at the Tax Appeals Tribunal. *(6 marks)*

*Model answer:* (See Section 4 — notice of appeal within 30 days; pay filing fee; lodge memorandum, statement of facts and tax decision within 14 days; serve Commissioner within 2 days; grounds limited to those pleaded; Tribunal determines within 90 days.)

**Q5.** State three grounds on which an appeal to the Court of Appeal in a tax matter may be founded. *(3 marks)*

*Model answer:* Decision contrary to law or established usage; failure to determine a material issue of law or usage; substantial procedural defect that may have caused a miscarriage of justice.

**Q6.** Define Alternative Dispute Resolution (ADR) in a tax context and state its statutory time frame under Section 55 of the TPA. *(3 marks)*

*Model answer:* ADR is a voluntary, facilitated mediation between a taxpayer and the Commissioner conducted outside TAT or the courts, in the presence of a mediator, aimed at settling a tax dispute. Section 55 requires cases pending before TAT or Court to be concluded within 90 days (courts may vary this).

**Q7.** State any four benefits of ADR over conventional litigation. *(4 marks)*

*Model answer:* Speedier resolution; lower cost; confidentiality; preservation of commercial relationship; negotiated (win–win) outcome; reduced court backlog; procedural flexibility. *(Any 4.)*

**Q8.** Identify the three parties to an ADR process and the role of the Facilitator. *(4 marks)*

*Model answer:* Parties: the taxpayer; the Commissioner (represented by relevant KRA departmental officers); the Facilitator. Facilitator's role: convene meetings, chair sittings and provide administrative guidance, attest to signing of the ADR Agreement, manage timelines.

**Q9.** List any three issues that are **excluded** from ADR. *(3 marks)*

*Model answer:* Matters whose settlement would be contrary to law/Constitution; disputes turning on technical interpretation of law; matters where public interest requires judicial clarification; matters covered by undisputed judgments; where one party is unwilling to engage. *(Any 3.)*

**Q10.** Explain the binding effect of a signed ADR agreement. *(3 marks)*

*Model answer:* The signed agreement represents the final position between the parties and operates as full and final settlement of the dispute; both parties retain a copy; any subsequent departure exposes the departing party to enforcement of the settled terms.

## 16. Exam technique and revision checklist

- Always quote the **statutory time limit** (30 days, 60 days, 90 days, 14 days, 2 days) — examiners commonly award marks per correctly stated timeline.
- Distinguish clearly between an **objection** (to the Commissioner) and an **appeal** (to TAT / High Court / Court of Appeal); using them interchangeably loses marks.
- On ADR questions, state that ADR is **voluntary** and **facilitated** — those two words carry weight.
- On procedural questions, list steps in **strict chronological order** and match them to the correct forum.
- When advising a taxpayer, always mention the **payment of the undisputed portion** — many candidates omit it and lose the "practical advice" marks.
- Case citations are optional; correct **principle** is compulsory.

**Quick revision — one line per stage:**
1. Objection to Commissioner within 30 days; Commissioner responds within 60 days.
2. Appeal to TAT within 30 days of objection decision; determined within 90 days.
3. Appeal to High Court within 30 days on points of law.
4. Appeal to Court of Appeal within 30 days on statutory grounds.
5. ADR is voluntary, facilitated, confidential; capped at 90 days for pending TAT/court matters.
6. Excluded from ADR: matters of technical legal interpretation and matters of public interest.
7. Signed ADR agreement = full and final settlement, binding on both parties.
