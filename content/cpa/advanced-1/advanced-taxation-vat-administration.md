## 1. What VAT administration covers

VAT is a **consumption tax** charged on the taxable supply of goods and services in Kenya, and on the importation of taxable goods and services into Kenya. "Administration" is the operational side of the tax — registration, invoicing, filing, payment, refund, and dispute — as opposed to pure computation.

**Three headline categories of supply:**
- **Standard-rated** — attracts VAT at the general rate (currently 16% for most goods and services; check the current Finance Act for the exact rate and for any special-rate sectors such as fuel).
- **Zero-rated** — a taxable supply charged at 0%. Input VAT on inputs used to make zero-rated supplies is **recoverable** (this is the key benefit of zero-rating over exemption).
- **Exempt** — outside the charge to VAT. Input VAT on inputs used exclusively for exempt supplies is **not recoverable**.

---

## 2. Obligations of a taxable person

A person becomes a "taxable person" once they are registered (or required to register) for VAT. The main obligations are:

- Register for VAT once turnover of taxable supplies crosses the statutory threshold (verify the current threshold in the Finance Act).
- Display the VAT registration certificate at a visible point in the business premises.
- Charge VAT on every taxable supply made.
- Issue serially numbered ETR (electronic tax register) tax invoices or receipts for every sale.
- Keep proper records of purchases, sales, credit notes, debit notes, and imports.
- File a monthly VAT return (VAT 3) online through iTax by the statutory due date (20th day of the following month).
- Pay any VAT due together with the return.
- Cooperate fully with authorised KRA officers during audits, verifications, or inspections.
- Settle any undisputed assessment immediately when raised.

Failure to comply exposes the taxpayer to penalties, interest, and audit reassessment.

## 3. Offences and penalties under the VAT Act

*Figures below are illustrative of the statutory framework — always confirm the current amount against the latest Finance Act because penalties are revised from time to time.*

| Offence | Consequence |
|---|---|
| Fraudulent refund claim | Penalty equal to **two times** the refund claimed |
| False statements, returns, or documents | Fine up to KSh 400,000 **or** double the tax evaded (whichever is greater), and/or up to 3 years' imprisonment |
| Failure to display registration certificate | Default penalty of KSh 20,000; plus fine up to KSh 200,000 and/or up to 2 years' imprisonment |
| Late registration | Default penalty of KSh 20,000 |
| Failure to issue a tax invoice | Default penalty between KSh 10,000 and KSh 200,000; related goods liable to forfeiture |
| Failure to keep records | Default penalty between KSh 10,000 and KSh 200,000 |
| Failure to file the monthly return | Penalty of KSh 10,000 **plus** interest of 2% per month, compounded, on tax due |
| ETR non-compliance | Fine up to KSh 500,000 and/or up to 3 years' imprisonment |
| General residual offence | Fine up to KSh 500,000 and/or up to 3 years' imprisonment |

## 4. Computing VAT payable: output vs input tax

Every month the registered person reports two totals on the VAT 3 return:

- **Output VAT** — VAT charged on taxable supplies made *to* customers.
- **Input VAT** — VAT paid on taxable supplies received *from* suppliers (including imports).

```
VAT payable = Output VAT − (Allowable) Input VAT
```

**If input VAT exceeds output VAT** the credit is **carried forward** to the next return, unless the excess is attributable to zero-rated supplies, in which case a **refund** may be claimed.

**Grossing up the VAT element:** for a VAT-inclusive figure at 16%, the VAT fraction is 16/116 (or equivalently 4/29). For example, VAT included in a KSh 58,000 gross figure = 58,000 × 4/29 = KSh 8,000.

## 5. Restriction of input tax (partial exemption)

Where a business makes *both* taxable and exempt supplies, input VAT is only fully deductible on inputs used exclusively for **taxable** supplies. For inputs used for **both** categories (overheads), input tax is apportioned.

**Standard apportionment formula:**

```
Deductible input tax = Total shared input VAT × (Taxable supplies ÷ Total supplies)
```

**De minimis rule:** if taxable supplies constitute more than **90%** of total supplies, the taxpayer may recover the **full** amount of shared input VAT (i.e. the restriction is switched off). Conversely, where taxable supplies fall below the lower statutory threshold, none of the shared input VAT is recoverable.

**Input tax that is never recoverable, regardless of use** (illustrative list — check the current Second Schedule / statutory list):
- Passenger cars and their maintenance (unless the business is car hire or dealing in vehicles).
- Entertainment, restaurant, and accommodation costs (unless in the business of supplying those services).
- Purchases without a valid ETR-generated tax invoice in the buyer's name.

## 6. Imported services and the reverse-charge rule

Normally, VAT is a liability of the *supplier*. But where a Kenyan registered person receives an **imported taxable service** from a supplier outside Kenya, the VAT liability is shifted to the **recipient** — this is the "**reverse charge**" mechanism.

**Practical operation:**
- The recipient computes VAT on the value of the imported service at the standard rate.
- The recipient accounts for that VAT as *output tax* on their next VAT return.
- Where the service is used in making taxable supplies, the recipient may in the same return claim the equivalent as *input tax* (net effect: nil).
- Where the service relates to exempt activity, no input claim is available — so the reverse-charge VAT is a real cost.

The purpose is competitive neutrality: local suppliers of the same service would have charged VAT, so imported services must be brought into charge too.

## 7. Withholding VAT agents

Certain persons — commonly government agencies, listed corporates, and other entities appointed by the Commissioner — are gazetted as **withholding VAT agents**. When they pay a VAT-registered supplier, they withhold a portion of the VAT at source and remit it directly to KRA.

**Mechanics:**
- The supplier still issues a normal tax invoice, showing full VAT at the applicable rate.
- The agent pays the net (invoice value − withheld VAT) to the supplier.
- The agent remits the withheld VAT to KRA and generates an electronic WHT-VAT certificate (VAT 32A) on iTax.
- The supplier uses that certificate as a credit against their output VAT when filing the next VAT 3 return.

Manual withholding certificates were discontinued — the entire process is now done through iTax.

---

## 8. VAT refunds and bad-debt relief

**A refund is a return of VAT already paid.** Circumstances giving rise to a refund include:

- Excess input VAT arising from making **zero-rated** supplies.
- VAT paid in **error** (claim must generally be lodged within 12 months of the error).
- VAT withheld by appointed withholding VAT agents that exceeds the supplier's output tax.
- VAT paid on goods that were subsequently exported under customs control before use.
- **Bad-debt relief** — VAT accounted for on a supply that has since become irrecoverable (subject to statutory conditions, typically requiring that the debt be at least a specified minimum age, that reasonable recovery efforts have been made, and that the claim be made within the statutory window).

**Preconditions for any refund claim:**
- The registered person must be **up to date** with all VAT return filings and payments.
- Where the amount claimed exceeds **KSh 1 million**, an auditor's / accountant's certificate must accompany the claim (see Section 9).

Where a refund is later found to have been paid in error, the Commissioner may demand repayment. Fraudulent claims attract a penalty of two times the amount claimed.

---

## 9. VAT accountant's / auditor's certificate

For refund claims **above KSh 1 million** (verify the current threshold), the claim must be supported by a certificate from an independent accountant/auditor confirming that:

- The claim is true and correctly computed.
- The amount is properly refundable under the VAT Act.
- Supporting records have been examined.

The purpose is to reduce fraudulent refund exposure and to shift a defensible portion of the burden of accuracy onto the certifying professional.

---

## 10. VAT automated assessments (VAA)

The **VAT Automated Assessment** is a system through which iTax cross-matches a taxpayer's declared input VAT with the *output VAT declared by the supplier* on the same transaction. Where a mismatch is detected — commonly because a supplier failed to declare the sale — the system generates an assessment notice against the *buyer*, disallowing the corresponding input VAT.

**Implications for taxpayers:**
- Only claim input VAT supported by an ETR invoice from a *compliant* supplier.
- Reconcile monthly purchases to supplier declarations before filing.
- Respond promptly to VAA notices — unaddressed mismatches convert to hard assessments that must then be objected to through the standard tax-dispute route.

---

## 11. iTax and its role in VAT administration

iTax is KRA's integrated online tax administration platform. For VAT it handles:

- PIN and VAT registration and de-registration.
- Monthly VAT 3 return filing.
- Generation of payment e-slips for use at appointed banks and mobile-money channels.
- Filing and payment of withholding VAT and issuance of WHT-VAT certificates.
- Refund applications and status tracking.
- VAA notices, objections, and taxpayer ledgers.

**Policy objectives behind iTax:**
- Simplify compliance for the taxpayer.
- Reduce time spent in physical KRA offices.
- Enable real-time visibility of taxpayer accounts.
- Improve accuracy through system-side validation.
- Enhance revenue collection through data-driven matching (e.g. VAA).

## 12. Case-law and interpretive practice

While VAT case law is highly fact-specific, a few principles recur in reported Kenyan tribunal and High Court decisions and should be quoted in exam answers where relevant:

- The onus is on the **taxpayer** to prove that a supply was zero-rated or exempt; where documentary evidence is missing, the Commissioner's standard-rated assessment tends to stand.
- Input VAT on **shared** costs must be apportioned; a taxpayer cannot claim 100% of shared input VAT merely because taxable supplies are its dominant activity, unless the de minimis rule applies.
- Reverse-charge VAT on imported services is a **strict statutory obligation** — failure to self-account triggers penalties even where the taxpayer would have been entitled to a full offsetting input claim.
- **Cite the principle even if unsure of the case citation.** Marks are awarded for correctly stating and applying the rule, not for remembering the exact case name and year.

## 13. Worked question — VAT payable with mixed supplies

**Scenario (original).** *Chai Bora Traders Ltd*, a VAT-registered wholesaler, presents the following data (all figures VAT-inclusive at 16% except where marked) for the month of March 2026:

- Standard-rated sales: KSh 4,640,000
- Zero-rated export sales: KSh 1,200,000 (VAT exclusive)
- Exempt sales: KSh 800,000
- Purchases of trading stock (all standard-rated, used across taxable and exempt lines): KSh 2,320,000
- Fuel and running costs for the delivery fleet (shared use): KSh 348,000
- Telephone and office overheads (shared use): KSh 116,000
- Legal fees for defending a taxable-supply contract: KSh 232,000

**Required:** Compute the VAT payable or refundable for March 2026.

**Model answer**

*Step 1 — Output VAT*
```
Standard-rated sales     4,640,000 × 4/29 = 640,000
Zero-rated exports       1,200,000 × 0%   =       0
Exempt sales                             not chargeable
                                          ---------
Total output VAT                            640,000
```

*Step 2 — Apportionment ratio for shared inputs*
```
Taxable supplies (excl. VAT):
  Standard-rated  = 4,640,000 × 25/29 = 4,000,000
  Zero-rated exports                 = 1,200,000
                                       ---------
                                       5,200,000
Exempt supplies                      =   800,000
Total supplies                       = 6,000,000

Taxable ÷ Total = 5,200,000 / 6,000,000 = 86.67%
```
Because the ratio is **below 90%**, the de minimis rule does *not* switch off the restriction — shared input VAT must be apportioned.

*Step 3 — Input VAT*
```
Trading stock (shared):
  2,320,000 × 4/29 = 320,000; recoverable 320,000 × 86.67% = 277,344
Fuel & fleet (shared):
    348,000 × 4/29 =  48,000; recoverable  48,000 × 86.67% =  41,602
Telephone & office (shared):
    116,000 × 4/29 =  16,000; recoverable  16,000 × 86.67% =  13,867
Legal fees (attributable exclusively to taxable supply):
    232,000 × 4/29 =  32,000; recoverable in full          =  32,000
                                                            --------
Total deductible input VAT                                   364,813
```

*Step 4 — VAT payable*
```
Output VAT                640,000
Less: Deductible input VAT (364,813)
                          ---------
VAT payable for March      275,187
```

**Answer:** VAT payable = **KSh 275,187**, due by 20 April 2026 through iTax.

## 14. Self-assessment question bank (original)

**Q1.** State any five obligations of a person registered for VAT. *(5 marks)*

*Model answer:* Register when threshold is crossed; display registration certificate; charge VAT on taxable supplies; issue serialised ETR tax invoices; keep proper records; file monthly VAT 3 return; pay VAT due by the statutory date; cooperate with KRA audits. *(Any 5.)*

**Q2.** Distinguish zero-rated supplies from exempt supplies. *(3 marks)*

*Model answer:* Zero-rated is a taxable supply charged at 0% with **full recovery of related input VAT**; exempt is *outside* the charge to VAT with **no recovery** of related input VAT. Consequence: a business making purely zero-rated supplies is in a structural refund position; a business making purely exempt supplies bears input VAT as a cost.

**Q3.** Explain the reverse-charge rule for imported services. *(4 marks)*

*Model answer:* Where a VAT-registered person in Kenya receives a taxable service from a supplier outside Kenya, the VAT liability shifts from the (foreign) supplier to the (Kenyan) recipient. The recipient self-accounts for VAT as output tax and, where the service is used for taxable business, claims an equal amount as input tax in the same return (net nil). Where the service supports exempt activity, the reverse-charge VAT is an unrecoverable cost. Purpose: preserve competitive neutrality with domestic suppliers.

**Q4.** State the standard input-tax apportionment formula and explain the 90% de minimis rule. *(3 marks)*

*Model answer:* Deductible shared input VAT = Total shared input VAT × (Taxable supplies / Total supplies). Where the ratio exceeds 90%, the taxpayer may recover 100% of shared input VAT without apportionment.

**Q5.** List three circumstances in which a VAT refund may be claimed. *(3 marks)*

*Model answer:* (i) Excess input arising from zero-rated supplies; (ii) VAT paid in error (claim within 12 months); (iii) Excess withholding VAT credits; (iv) VAT on goods exported before use; (v) Bad-debt relief. *(Any 3.)*

**Q6.** When is a VAT accountant's/auditor's certificate required? *(2 marks)*

*Model answer:* When the amount of a refund claim exceeds KSh 1 million (verify current threshold). It must confirm that the claim is true, properly computed, and refundable under the VAT Act.

**Q7.** State four functions performed through the iTax platform in VAT administration. *(4 marks)*

*Model answer:* VAT registration; filing monthly VAT 3 returns; generation of payment e-slips; withholding VAT filing and certificate issuance; refund applications; VAA notice management. *(Any 4.)*

**Q8.** Explain what triggers a VAA notice and outline the taxpayer's practical response. *(4 marks)*

*Model answer:* A VAA notice is triggered when iTax detects that input VAT claimed by a buyer does not match output VAT declared by the supplier on the corresponding transaction. Response: (i) reconcile ledger to supplier's declaration; (ii) contact the non-compliant supplier and require them to file/correct their return; (iii) submit supporting ETR invoices to KRA; (iv) if unresolved, lodge a formal objection within the statutory window to prevent the assessment crystallising.

**Q9.** State any three offences relating to VAT and the associated penalty range. *(6 marks)*

*Model answer:* (Any three — see the table in Section 3.)

**Q10.** Explain the mechanics of the withholding VAT system and its effect on the supplier's monthly return. *(5 marks)*

*Model answer:* Appointed agents deduct a portion of VAT when paying VAT-registered suppliers, remit it to KRA, and generate a WHT-VAT certificate (VAT 32A) on iTax. The supplier still declares the full output VAT on the VAT 3 return but claims the certificate as a credit, so only the un-withheld balance of output VAT is paid over in cash. This shifts collection risk from KRA to large, compliant payers.

## 15. Exam technique and revision checklist

- Always **separate output VAT from input VAT** before netting — examiners deduct marks for jumping straight to a net figure.
- When apportionment applies, **show the ratio calculation** and state whether the 90% rule is or is not triggered.
- For refund questions, always state the **precondition** that all returns must be up to date.
- Do not treat every excess of input over output as automatically refundable — only zero-rated-driven excesses trigger a refund; others *carry forward*.
- Convert VAT-inclusive figures using **4/29** at 16%, not by dividing by 1.16 in the head — mark the arithmetic on the script.
- On offence questions, quote both the **financial penalty** and any **imprisonment term**; either alone loses marks.

**One-line revision recap:**
1. Register when threshold crossed; keep records; file monthly by the 20th.
2. VAT payable = Output − Deductible Input.
3. Zero-rated ≠ Exempt; only zero-rated preserves input recovery.
4. Restrict shared input VAT when taxable share ≤ 90% of total supplies.
5. Reverse-charge shifts VAT on imported services to the Kenyan recipient.
6. Withholding VAT: agent pays part of VAT to KRA; supplier credits certificate.
7. Refunds need up-to-date returns; over KSh 1m needs an accountant's certificate.
8. VAA disallows input VAT where the supplier hasn't declared the sale.
9. iTax is the single channel — registration, returns, payments, certificates, refunds.
10. Penalties: fraudulent refund = 2× claim; false returns = up to KSh 400k or 2× tax evaded plus jail.
